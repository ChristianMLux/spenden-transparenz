# Case Study: GlobalGiving — Vetting, Field Reports & Last-Mile Verification Mechanics

Research date: 2026-08-28. All facts below carry a source URL. Where no source could be found, this is stated explicitly as "not found." Anything not directly stated on a GlobalGiving (or third-party) page is marked **[INFERENCE]**.

---

## 1. Vetting / Due Diligence

**Documents required.** GlobalGiving collects and reviews "legal documents, financial records, program materials, and lists of senior staff and board members" to validate organizational legitimacy and confirm the org is running the project as described ([Beyond The Checklist: How We Vet Nonprofits](https://www.globalgiving.org/learn/how-globalgiving-vets-nonprofits/); [How can I be sure a project is legitimate?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026413511-How-can-I-be-sure-a-project-is-legitimate)).

The exact document checklist differs by country of registration ([Nonprofit Application Documents + Next Steps](https://www.globalgiving.org/nonprofit-application-documents/)):

- **US organizations:** IRS Employer Identification Number (EIN) — this can auto-populate 501(c)(3) status via a linked GuideStar/Candid profile, or the org must upload an IRS Letter of Determination, a recent Form 990, or two years of financial statements; program materials; bank disbursement info; names of senior staff and board members; and acceptance of GlobalGiving's Terms and Conditions.
- **International organizations:** program materials (with English translation if the originals aren't in English); a **letter of reference** from someone who has seen or experienced the org's work (a funder or community partner — explicitly *not* family, staff, or board members), dated within the last 3 months, addressed to GlobalGiving, with the referee's contact details; a **certificate of government registration** proving charitable status, issued by a government office; a **founding document with a dissolution clause** (constitution/bylaws), stamped by a government agency or signed by board members; **two years of financial statements** (audited or unaudited) plus a current-year budget; bank account info in the organization's name; names of senior staff/board members; and Terms and Conditions acceptance.

**Countries covered.** GlobalGiving vets nonprofits across **175+ countries**, requiring each to "meet local requirements for registration with their local government" ([Vetting — GlobalGiving](https://www.globalgiving.org/aboutus/how-it-works/vetting/), fetched via proxy).

**Compliance checks.** Beyond document review, GlobalGiving checks compliance with "anti-terror guidelines and international guidelines for philanthropy," evaluates organizational capacity to implement and communicate about its work, and researches the organization's relationship with previous funders ([Beyond The Checklist](https://www.globalgiving.org/learn/how-globalgiving-vets-nonprofits/); [How can I be sure a project is legitimate?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026413511-How-can-I-be-sure-a-project-is-legitimate)). GlobalGiving frames its approach as evaluating the **whole organization**, not just the specific project applying for a page — decisions are described as human-judgment-driven ("computers and automated systems complement but don't replace human intelligence or decision-making"), not a pure document checklist ([Beyond The Checklist](https://www.globalgiving.org/learn/how-globalgiving-vets-nonprofits/)).

**Timeline.** Vetting decisions are typically made **within 2–3 weeks** of a complete application ([Beyond The Checklist](https://www.globalgiving.org/learn/how-globalgiving-vets-nonprofits/)).

**In-person vs. virtual check.** The core application review is document-based / virtual, not an inherent in-person requirement. However, GlobalGiving's "How It Works" overview states the organization has "conducted site visits for most organizations — even those in the remote Amazon!" ([How It Works](https://www.globalgiving.org/aboutus/how-it-works/), fetched via proxy) — see Section 3 for how this in-person layer (Field Travelers / staff / ambassadors) operates; it is presented as largely separate from initial application approval, not a mandatory gate for every org before its first project goes live. **[INFERENCE]**: the "for most organizations" phrasing implies site visits are common but not confirmed as 100%-universal or pre-approval-mandatory.

**Re-vetting frequency.** GlobalGiving performs ongoing monitoring (reviewing reports, activity, and community feedback) plus a **comprehensive due-diligence renewal at least every two years** ("Upon expiry of the initial due diligence and at a minimum every two years thereafter, GlobalGiving will undertake any further checks it deems necessary to re-verify the identity and objectives of such nonprofit") ([How can I be sure a project is legitimate?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026413511-How-can-I-be-sure-a-project-is-legitimate); corroborated in [Nonprofit Terms + Conditions](https://www.globalgiving.org/aboutus/terms-and-conditions/), per search snippet).

**Rejection rate.** Not found — GlobalGiving does not appear to publish an application rejection/acceptance percentage. One third-party aggregation states GlobalGiving has "vetted over 6,000 organizations cumulatively" as of FY2022 and recommends donations across "9,000+ pre-screened projects," but this describes the vetted pool, not a rejection rate (source: search-engine synthesis citing [GlobalGiving Nonprofits page](https://www.globalgiving.org/nonprofits/) and [Beyond The Checklist](https://www.globalgiving.org/learn/how-globalgiving-vets-nonprofits/) — could not independently verify the 6,000/9,000 figures against a primary GlobalGiving page during this research; flagged as **lower-confidence**).

## 2. Project Reports

**Mandatory, and at what frequency.** Every project must post an update **at least once every three months** ("Organizations must post an update for each project 'at least once every three months.'") ([What are GlobalGiving's reporting requirements?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026175992-What-are-GlobalGiving-s-reporting-requirements)).

**What happens if a report is missed.** GlobalGiving first issues a **warning**. If the organization continues not to meet the requirement, the consequence is **the project being removed from search results on the site** — i.e., the project is de-indexed/hidden from discovery rather than immediately deleted ([What are GlobalGiving's reporting requirements?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026175992-What-are-GlobalGiving-s-reporting-requirements)).

**What a report contains.** The source pages describe reports as covering "activities, accomplishments and results of the project to-date" ([How can I be sure a project is legitimate?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026413511-How-can-I-be-sure-a-project-is-legitimate); [What are GlobalGiving's reporting requirements?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026175992-What-are-GlobalGiving-s-reporting-requirements)). The exact required fields (word count, photo requirement, numeric metrics) were **not found** in the pages retrieved — the support article does not itemize report structure beyond "post an update."

**Distribution.** Reports are **emailed to donors** and **published publicly on the project page** ("These reports are emailed to donors and publicly available on the project page so supporters can see how their donation is being used") — per search-engine excerpt of GlobalGiving support content; a direct fetch of the same support article could not confirm the "public" wording verbatim (see note above), so this point carries **medium confidence**, sourced to: [What are GlobalGiving's reporting requirements?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026175992-What-are-GlobalGiving-s-reporting-requirements).

**Reporting frequency feeds ranking.** GlobalGiving's search-ranking algorithm factors in GG Rewards status/points, reporting frequency, donor count, and donation volume — i.e., more frequent reporting improves a nonprofit's visibility on the platform (search-engine synthesis of [GlobalGiving support content](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026175992-What-are-GlobalGiving-s-reporting-requirements)). Timely reporting also earns **Engagement Points** within GG Rewards (Section 3) ([What is GG Rewards?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026176852-What-is-GG-Rewards), per search snippet).

## 3. Field Verification

**Field Travelers program.** GlobalGiving employs "Field Travelers" — hired, trained, and directed from GlobalGiving's Washington, DC office — who travel internationally for **three to six months** per assignment, in a specific country or region ([Field Travelers — GlobalGiving](https://www.globalgiving.org/aboutus/who-we-are/field-travelers/), per search snippet; page content not independently retrievable via proxy on this pass — see note below).

**What they check / do.** Per the role description surfaced via search: Field Travelers "verify nonprofit partners' projects in person through **20–50 day-long site visits** to their program sites," support current partners through 5–10 in-person training sessions, recruit new organizations via 5–10 workshops, and build relationships with nonprofit networks ([Field Travelers — GlobalGiving](https://www.globalgiving.org/aboutus/who-we-are/field-travelers/), search snippet; also referenced in job posting language at [jobgallery.com listing](https://www.jobgallery.com/long-term-globalgiving-field-traveler_washington-dc_40771.html), a third-party recruiting mirror of GlobalGiving's own posting).

**Program scale (cumulative, all-time).** Field Travelers have visited **more than 60 countries**, performed **over 1,000 site visits**, across six continents, over the life of the program (search-engine synthesis, sourced to GlobalGiving's own Field Travelers page and the [12 Things GlobalGiving Field Travelers Learned In 2017](https://medium.com/globalgoodness/12-things-globalgiving-field-travelers-learned-in-2017-1a61f88b2289) blog post). No current-year (2026) site-visit count was found.

**GPS / photo-evidence requirement.** Not found. No page retrieved specifies a formal GPS-tagging or standardized photo-evidence protocol for site visits; the visits appear to be qualitative in-person checks (talking to staff/beneficiaries, observing programs) rather than a documented evidentiary standard. **[INFERENCE]**: given the "verify... in person" language and the badge name "Site Visit Verified" (below), some photo documentation likely exists in practice, but this is not confirmed in any retrieved primary source.

**Badges.** GlobalGiving's recognition/trust badges, per [GG Rewards FAQ](https://www.globalgiving.org/gg-rewards-faq/) and support-article search snippets:

- **Vetted** — automatically applied to every organization at Partner, Leader, or Superstar GG Rewards status, since all of these have passed GlobalGiving's due-diligence process (search snippet citing GlobalGiving support content; underlying due-diligence process is Section 1).
- **Site Visit Verified** — "means one of GlobalGiving's field travelers, staff members, or ambassadors has visited [the] organization and verified the work they're doing" (search-engine synthesis of GlobalGiving support/FAQ content; specific support-article URL not confirmed reachable in this pass, but the exact quoted definition recurred consistently across two independent search queries).
- **Effective Nonprofit** — awarded once an org has earned **at least 12 Effectiveness Points** in GG Rewards (search snippet; see below for how Effectiveness Points are earned).
- **Top Ranked** — for Superstar-status orgs showing high community engagement and effectiveness commitment (search snippet).
- **Staff Favorite** / **Project of the Month** — editorial/marketing badges (staff picks; monthly matching-fund selections) (search snippet), not evidentiary verification badges.

**GG Rewards point system.** Two point types ([What is GG Rewards?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026176852-What-is-GG-Rewards); [How are GG Rewards Statuses Determined?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026176912-How-are-GG-Rewards-Statuses-Determined)):
- **Engagement Points** — earned by platform activity: fundraising through the site, writing on-time project reports, thanking donors, participating in campaigns/academies, keeping org info current.
- **Effectiveness Points** — earned by "growing your organization's impact by listening to feedback from your community members, acting on that information, and demonstrating how you've learned from your actions."
- Status tiers, based on combined points tracked over the **trailing 12 months**: **Partner = 0–17 points, Leader = 18–35 points, Superstar = 36+ points.** Points are calculated automatically; no manual logging.
- How Engagement vs. Effectiveness points are weighted/combined into the single point total used for tier thresholds was **not found** in the retrieved pages.

## 4. Disaster Funds Mechanics

**The 2026 Nepal Flood Relief Fund.** Following catastrophic flash floods that swept the Bhote Koshi river system in Nepal's Rasuwa district starting **August 26, 2026**, GlobalGiving launched a fund with a **$1.5 million goal** ([Nepal Flood Relief Fund](https://www.globalgiving.org/projects/nepal-flood-relief-fund/), fetched 2026-08-28). At time of this research (2 days after launch): **$174,819 raised from 1,433 donors**, toward the $1.5M goal (same source, live figures as of 2026-08-28).

**Fund usage / mechanics stated on the page:** the fund provides "local organizations with flexible funding to meet urgent needs, including search and rescue, emergency medical care, shelter, food, clean water, and other essential supplies," then transitions to "longer-term recovery and rebuilding, especially for marginalized, remote, and hard-to-reach communities" ([Nepal Flood Relief Fund](https://www.globalgiving.org/projects/nepal-flood-relief-fund/)). No specific grantee organizations are named on the fund page as of this research date; funds are described only as going to "local organizations" and "trusted local leaders." **Timing of first grants** (days vs. weeks) was **not found** on this page.

**General disaster-fund model (pattern across funds).** GlobalGiving runs the same structure for each disaster (Nepal, Mexico, Italy, Ecuador, Indonesia/Lombok, Japan/Kumamoto, Venezuela, Colombia, etc. — see [Disaster Response](https://www.globalgiving.org/disasters/)): money is first directed to meet the most urgent/immediate needs (shelter, medical care, food, clean water), and once initial relief work is complete, funds shift to longer-term recovery led by local organizations. All partner organizations receiving disaster funds are described as vetted (search-engine synthesis of GlobalGiving disaster-fund project pages).

**Fees on disaster funds — this is where GlobalGiving's disaster-fund fee structure differs from standard project fees:**
- **Disaster funds:** 12% nonprofit-support fee + 3% payment-processing fee = **15% total** ([What is the fee on GlobalGiving's disaster funds?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360033823391-What-is-the-fee-on-GlobalGiving-s-disaster-funds)).
- **Standard individual projects (not disaster funds):** nonprofit-support fee of **12% on an organization's first $5,000 raised**, dropping to **5% (US/UK-registered orgs)** or **7% (internationally registered orgs)** after that, plus the same 3% processing fee — so **8–10% total** on ongoing fundraising past the first $5,000 ([GlobalGiving's Fee](https://www.globalgiving.org/aboutus/fee/)).
- GlobalGiving states "a minimum of 85¢ of every $1 donated goes directly to our nonprofit partners" and claims that for every $1 spent on fees, it raises an additional $2 for partner organizations via matching/marketing (same source).
- **[INFERENCE]** The higher disaster-fund fee (12% vs. 5–7%) likely reflects the additional staff cost of rapid vetting/onboarding of new local responders under time pressure — this rationale is stated qualitatively ("staff costs for identifying and vetting organizations... ongoing support to strengthen community-led organizations for future crises") but not quantified ([What is the fee on GlobalGiving's disaster funds?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360033823391-What-is-the-fee-on-GlobalGiving-s-disaster-funds)).

**The 2015 Nepal Earthquake Relief Fund — retrospective numbers and lessons.**
Source: [Reports on Nepal Earthquake Relief and Recovery Fund](https://www.globalgiving.org/projects/nepal-earthquake-relief-fund/reports/), plus corroborating search-engine synthesis of the same and related report pages.

- **Total raised:** $5.1 million from **over 44,000 donors**.
- **Number of local nonprofit partners:** **86 vetted organizations** initially reported, growing to **89 partners** by a January 2018 update.
- **Context of the disaster:** the April 2015 earthquake killed nearly 9,000 people, destroyed over 500,000 homes, and left an estimated 2.8–3.5 million people needing assistance.
- **Speed of local response (one grantee example):** the partner organization **Tewa** deployed a network of 22 Nepali women ("Shadow Barefoot Volunteers") who hand-delivered cash grants in $100 batches directly to survivors, reaching **120 communities across 15 affected districts within 70 days** of the earthquake — described as "often delivering the first wave of relief in struggling remote villages" (this is the clearest concrete timing data point found for how fast a GlobalGiving-funded local partner reached beneficiaries).
- **Recovery-phase results reported:** 20 earthquake-safe classrooms rebuilt directly; over 36,000 classrooms had been damaged/destroyed platform-wide (nearly 1 million children were out of school as a result); school toilets/water facilities, immunizations, and eye exams delivered under health/sanitation projects; anti-trafficking and child-labor-rescue support; women's economic-empowerment projects (garden restoration generating $10–15/day income for widows).
- **Lesson learned (coordination):** a documented lesson from the broader international response (not GlobalGiving-specific, but referenced in GlobalGiving's own reporting context) was that "relief organizations were distributing relief materials randomly and in an uncoordinated manner, which resulted in unequal distribution" — underscoring the value of coordinated, locally-embedded partners over ad hoc external actors.
- **Approach emphasized in retrospective reporting:** community-led, locally-driven recovery over top-down international intervention, with an explicit emphasis on women's leadership roles in rebuilding.

I could not locate a single, formally-titled "learning report" or after-action PDF specifically for the 2015 Nepal fund (e.g., a "State of the Fund" or lessons-learned whitepaper) — the retrospective data above is assembled from the fund's ongoing report stream on globalgiving.org, not one consolidated document. **Marked as not found**: a discrete downloadable "2015 Nepal Earthquake Fund learning report" PDF.

## 5. IATI Publishing

GlobalGiving is registered on the IATI Registry as publisher **"globalgiving,"** IATI identifier prefix **US-EIN-300108263**, headquartered in the US, with a **first publication date of July 30, 2012** and contact email help@globalgiving.org (search-engine synthesis of [IATI Registry publisher page](https://iatiregistry.org/publisher/globalgiving) and [IATI Organization Dataset](https://iatiregistry.org/dataset/globalgiving-org) — the live iatiregistry.org publisher page itself now 404s with a migration notice pointing to the new "IATI Account" system, per direct fetch on 2026-08-28).

**What is published and how current.** Live data pulled from **d-portal.org** on 2026-08-28 shows ([d-portal ctrack — publisher US-EIN-300108263](https://d-portal.org/ctrack.html?publisher=US-EIN-300108263)):
- **27,015 total activities** published.
- Of these, **7,070 are shown as "Currently Implementing"** and **19,945 in "Finalisation Stage"**; by simple present/past status, **7,038 "present" (active) vs. 19,972 "past" (ended)**, and **0 marked "future" (planned)**.
- **9,426 participating organisations** referenced across GlobalGiving's published activity set (this counts organisation-roles across activities, not unique nonprofits necessarily).
- Sample ended activities carry individual **commitment amounts** in USD (e.g., "Emergency COVID-19 support for Cambodian families," $15,208; "Help Nepali Children Walk on Their Own Two Feet," $40,247), both shown ending October 16, 2023 — indicating GlobalGiving publishes at the **individual project/activity level**, not just organization-level aggregates, and includes **commitment-type transaction data**.
- **Data currency:** the d-portal database itself was last updated **August 28, 2026, 07:26 GMT** (i.e., same-day as this research) — consistent with an earlier search-engine finding that GlobalGiving "updates its IATI data once every day" via feed at `www.globalgiving.org/iati/` (per search snippet referencing the IATI Registry dataset page).
- **Data-quality gap flagged by d-portal itself:** "0% of activities have provided exact locations" — i.e., geographic/GPS-level location data is not being published per-activity, despite the large activity count.
- A separate discovery attempt via the **IATI Dashboard** ([dashboard.iatistandard.org/publisher/globalgiving.html](https://dashboard.iatistandard.org/publisher/globalgiving.html)) returned a 403 and could not be retrieved in this session — so official IATI Standard "timeliness/comprehensiveness/forward-looking" quality scores (which the Dashboard normally reports) are **not found** here.
- An earlier IATI Registry snapshot (via search-engine synthesis, exact date unclear — likely 2025) had reported **23,124 activities** with a **content-length download error noted June 11, 2025**; the newer d-portal count (27,015, pulled live today) supersedes this but the discrepancy is noted for transparency about source recency.

**Bottom line on IATI:** GlobalGiving publishes **per-project/per-activity** data (not just organization-level totals), refreshed **daily**, including transaction-level commitment amounts — but published records are essentially devoid of geolocation data (0% with exact locations per d-portal), and this research could not confirm whether disbursement-level (as opposed to commitment-level) transactions are systematically published, nor whether IATI data links back to the specific narrative project reports described in Section 2.

## 6. Sourced Statements vs. Inference — What Is *Last-Mile* vs. *Compliance* Verification

**True last-mile verification (some evidence something actually happened on the ground):**
- **Field Traveler / staff / ambassador site visits** ("Site Visit Verified" badge) — a person is physically present, observes the project, and the badge is applied per-organization. This is the closest GlobalGiving gets to ground-truth verification, but it is episodic (not tied to every disbursement) and the program's current-year visit cadence and any photo/GPS evidence standard were **not found** — so even this is closer to "someone from GlobalGiving has been there at some point" than "this specific tranche of money produced this specific outcome."
- **Project reports with narrative + (implied) photos**, required every 3 months and emailed to donors — these are self-reported by the grantee organization, not independently audited by GlobalGiving per report. **[INFERENCE]**: GlobalGiving's "ongoing monitoring... reviewing reports, activity, and community feedback" ([How can I be sure a project is legitimate?](https://support.globalgiving.org/hc/en-us/articles/360026413511-How-can-I-be-sure-a-project-is-legitimate)) suggests some spot-checking against self-reports, but no confirmed independent-audit mechanism per report was found.
- **Concrete outcome-timing data** exists in retrospective reporting (e.g., Tewa's 70-day, 120-community cash-grant distribution after the 2015 Nepal earthquake) — but this is drawn from the grantee's own report, published by GlobalGiving, not independently field-verified in that specific instance as far as retrieved sources show.

**Compliance verification (evidence the org exists, is legally registered, and reports on schedule — not evidence of on-the-ground impact):**
- The entire initial vetting/due-diligence flow (Section 1): government registration certificates, financial statements, reference letters, anti-terror compliance, GuideStar/IRS 990 cross-referencing. This confirms the organization is *real and legally compliant*, not that any specific project delivered any specific result.
- **GG Rewards Engagement Points** — largely measure platform activity (reporting on time, thanking donors, participating in campaigns), which is a proxy for *diligence/responsiveness*, not for impact.
- **IATI publishing** (Section 5) — this is fundamentally a **compliance/transparency-format** exercise: it proves GlobalGiving is publishing standardized, machine-readable, dated activity and commitment data on time, but (per the 0%-exact-location gap) does **not** currently prove where on the ground the money went, and (unconfirmed) may not show whether funds were actually **disbursed** vs. merely **committed**.
- **Vetted / Effective Nonprofit / Top Ranked badges** are almost entirely compliance- and engagement-based (documentation on file, points earned by platform behavior), not outcome-verification badges — with the partial exception of "Site Visit Verified."

**What a transparency platform could reuse or link to instead of rebuilding [INFERENCE — synthesis for the requesting project]:**
- GlobalGiving's **document-checklist + reference-letter + biennial re-vetting** model (Section 1) is a well-specified, replicable compliance layer that a new platform could adopt largely as-is rather than re-inventing (registration certificate + dissolution-clause founding document + 2-year financials + independent reference letter, refreshed every 2 years).
- GlobalGiving's **IATI feed** is a public, standardized, daily-updated data source (27,015 activities, machine-readable, commitment-level) that a transparency platform could **query/link to directly** for any GlobalGiving-funded organization, rather than re-collecting the same compliance data — though it should be flagged to users that IATI here reflects *commitments and reporting*, not verified *outcomes* or *locations*.
- The **"Site Visit Verified" badge concept** is the one piece that GlobalGiving cannot hand off via API/feed (it's an internal HR/logistics program — Field Travelers hired and run from a DC office), so if genuine field-level, GPS/photo-backed last-mile verification is the goal, that is the piece a new platform would have to build itself rather than reuse from GlobalGiving.
