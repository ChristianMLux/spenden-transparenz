Nepal Flash Flood: Top-Rated Charities Providing Relief

    Aug 27, 2026

A catastrophic flash flood has devastated communities along the Nepal-Tibet border, killing hundreds of people and leaving more than 1,000 missing as search-and-rescue operations continue.

The disaster struck on August 26, 2026, when a massive collapse of ice and rock high in the Himalayas sent a destructive surge of water, mud, and debris through river valleys in northern Nepal. Initial reports suggested that an earthquake may have triggered the disaster, but the U.S. Geological Survey later determined that the seismic activity detected in the region was generated by the collapse itself rather than an earthquake. Satellite analysis indicates that an ice-and-rock avalanche entered a tributary of the Bhote Koshi/Trishuli river system near the Tibet border, producing the devastating downstream flood.

The scale of the destruction is still emerging. Entire villages and market areas have been swept away or buried in mud and debris. Roads, bridges, homes, and hydropower infrastructure have been destroyed, leaving some affected communities extremely difficult to reach. As of August 27, at least 270 deaths had been reported and nearly 1,400 people were missing across Nepal and Tibet, including hundreds of foreign tourists and pilgrims. Those numbers are expected to change as rescue workers reach isolated areas and authorities account for the missing.

Nepal’s mountainous terrain and the destruction of transportation infrastructure are complicating rescue and relief efforts. Helicopters are being used to evacuate survivors, while humanitarian organizations are working with Nepalese authorities and local partners to assess urgent needs for medical care, shelter, clean water, sanitation, food, and other emergency supplies.

The United States has announced $500,000 in immediate humanitarian assistance for flood-affected communities. The assistance is being provided through Catholic Relief Services and is expected to support emergency shelter, relief supplies, and water, sanitation, and hygiene services. The U.S. is also deploying a disaster-response adviser to Nepal.

CharityWatch Top-Rated Charities Providing Assistance

CharityWatch announces a list of highly rated charities involved in relief and recovery efforts for people affected by the Nepal flash flood. Donors who want to help should consider supporting charities that already have operations, established partners, or disaster-response capacity in the affected region.

All Hands And Hearts

All Hands and Hearts reports that it is on the ground responding to the floods and mudslides in Nuwakot, Nepal. The organization has maintained a presence in Nepal since the devastating 2015 earthquake, including work rebuilding schools in the region.

CharityWatch rates All Hands and Hearts A+, with 92% of its cash budget spent on programs and a cost of $3 to raise each $100 in contributions.

Donate or learn more from All Hands and Hearts

Catholic Relief Services

Catholic Relief Services is coordinating with Caritas Nepal and local partners to assess needs and provide assistance to affected communities. The U.S. government has announced that $500,000 in emergency assistance will be provided through CRS to support emergency shelter, relief items, and water, sanitation, and hygiene assistance.

CharityWatch rates Catholic Relief Services A+, with 92% of its cash budget spent on programs and a cost of $9 to raise each $100 in contributions.

Catholic Relief Services Nepal Flood Relief

CARE

CharityWatch rates CARE USA, a U.S.-based member of the CARE International confederation. CARE USA helps fund and coordinate humanitarian programs across the CARE network, including work carried out through CARE Nepal.

CARE Nepal is coordinating with Nepal’s government, disaster-management authorities, humanitarian organizations, and local partners to assess needs in affected communities. CARE says it is preparing to mobilize emergency assistance including shelter, hygiene, and dignity kits as needs are confirmed.

CharityWatch rates CARE A-, with 89% of its cash budget spent on programs and a cost of $18 to raise each $100 in contributions.

Learn more about CARE’s Nepal response

Direct Relief

Direct Relief is mobilizing medical assistance in response to the disaster and is communicating with Nepal’s Ministry of Health and nonprofit partners in the country. The organization has provided an emergency operating grant to Mountain Heart Nepal to mobilize emergency medical teams for deployment to flood-affected communities. Those teams have access to Direct Relief field medic packs that had already been positioned in Nepal before the disaster.

CharityWatch rates Direct Relief & Direct Relief Foundation A+, with 90% of its cash budget spent on programs and a cost of $5 to raise each $100 in contributions.

Direct Relief’s Nepal Flash Flood Response

Mercy Corps

Mercy Corps, which has worked in Nepal since 2005, is coordinating with local authorities and humanitarian partners to determine the most urgent needs. The organization reports that immediate priorities include medical care, safe shelter, clean water, food, and essential supplies for survivors, while damaged roads and bridges are making some communities difficult to reach.

CharityWatch rates Mercy Corps A, with 82% of its cash budget spent on programs and a cost of $7 to raise each $100 in contributions.

Learn more about Mercy Corps’ Nepal response

United States Fund For UNICEF

CharityWatch rates United States Fund for UNICEF, the U.S.-based 501(c)(3) organization that raises funds to support UNICEF’s work worldwide. Funds raised by the U.S. charity are granted to UNICEF and other nonprofit organizations to support UNICEF-assisted programs, including emergency relief; UNICEF’s established country operations and personnel then carry out humanitarian programs on the ground.

UNICEF is responding to the humanitarian needs of children and families affected by the flooding and has launched appeals related to the Nepal emergency. UNICEF’s disaster-response work can include safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene supplies, medical care, nutrition assistance, and protection for children affected by emergencies.

CharityWatch rates United States Fund for UNICEF, also known as UNICEF USA, A-, with 81% of its cash budget spent on programs and a cost of $17 to raise each $100 in contributions.

Give Wisely

Major disasters often generate an immediate outpouring of generosity. With this comes opportunities for scammers to exploit donors’ desire to help. CharityWatch recommends that donors avoid responding impulsively to unsolicited emails, text messages, social-media posts, or crowdfunding appeals unless they can independently verify who is collecting the money and how it will be used.

Whenever possible, donate directly through a charity’s official website rather than clicking on links in unsolicited messages. Donors should also understand that charities responding to disasters may use contributions designated for a particular emergency for immediate relief, longer-term recovery, or other related humanitarian needs, depending on the terms of the charity’s solicitation.

The destruction in Nepal is extensive, and recovery is likely to continue long after the disaster disappears from the headlines. Donors who want to help can support experienced organizations already operating in Nepal that are equipped to respond both to immediate humanitarian needs and the longer-term work of rebuilding affected communities.
